INA228 Precision Power Monitor - V1

A standalone in-line power measurement module that sits between a UAV's battery and its power distribution board, measuring real current, voltage, power, and accumulated energy in flight - built to turn a spec-sheet endurance number into measured flight data.

INA228 24-bit I²C power monitor
1mΩ High-side Kelvin-sensed shunt
XT90 ~90A in-line connectors

Overview

The disaster-response UAV claims 22-minute endurance and a 5kg payload - currently a spec sheet number, not a measured one. This board is an in-line power monitor that sits directly between the battery and the power distribution board, using a TI INA228 to measure real current, voltage, power, and accumulated energy during flight, so that claim can be validated against real flight data instead of taken on faith. It's also a deliberate exercise in precision analog measurement, high-side current sensing, and lab-grade validation - the kind of work that sits behind most analog and power IC roles.

Functional Overview

Sensing Architecture

  • TI INA228: a 24-bit high-side current/voltage/power monitor with a hardware energy and charge accumulator - real coulomb counting on-chip, not software integration of sampled current
  • High-side topology: the shunt sits directly in the battery's positive line, with the differential sense path branching off it carrying essentially zero current
  • I²C interface to an external MCU. V1 talks to a Nucleo F441 over jumper wires - there's no onboard MCU, which keeps the new engineering risk isolated to the analog front end

Shunt & ADC Range Sizing

  • Shunt: ASR-K-7-1G, 1mΩ, 9W, ±2%, ±50ppm/°C, 5930 case - 80mV at 80A, about 49% of full scale, leaving headroom for current spikes
  • ADCRANGE = 0 (±163.84mV full scale) - ADCRANGE = 1 would clip at roughly 41A, well under the peak this board needs to capture
  • CURRENT_LSB ≈ 152.6µA/bit, derived as 80A / 2¹⁹, with SHUNT_CAL ≈ 2000 (13107.2×10⁶ × CURRENT_LSB × R_shunt)
  • 80A and 40–50A are answers to two different questions: 80A is a 1–2 second peak, so the shunt value and ADC range are sized against it to avoid clipping; the copper pour is sized against the lower sustained thermal load instead

Signal Filtering & Layout

  • 10Ω + 10Ω + 1µF differential input filter ahead of the INA228's sense pins, giving roughly an 8kHz cutoff that rejects ESC switching noise
  • Power pour sized 24mm wide in 2oz copper against a 70A rating, well above the real sustained load of 40–50A
  • Kelvin sensing: R2 is a 2-terminal part, so sense quality is entirely a layout problem - sense traces tap at the very edge of R2's pads, symmetric, equal length, 0.25mm, and routed clear of the power pour
  • Single continuous GND pour on both layers, stitched with vias; the INA228's local reference sits away from the main battery-in-to-PDB-out return current corridor

PCB Design Highlights

  • Two-layer, high-current in-line topology with a 24mm/2oz power pour sized against sustained load and a shunt/ADC range independently sized against instantaneous peak
  • Kelvin-sensed shunt connection: symmetric, equal-length, 0.25mm sense traces tapped at the edge of the shunt pads and routed clear of the power pour
  • Single continuous stitched GND pour with the precision-analog reference deliberately kept out of the main high-current return path
  • Clean ERC and DRC verification prior to fabrication
